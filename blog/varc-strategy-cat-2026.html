VARC Strategy for CAT 2026: Reading Comprehension & Verbal Ability Tips

Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

VARC quietly decides a lot of CAT results. It carries roughly 24 of the ~68 total questions — around a third of your overall score — yet most aspirants with an engineering or math background under-prepare for it simply because it feels less "learnable" than QA. It isn't. VARC rewards structured practice just as much as formulas do.

How to Approach Reading Comprehension

RC passages typically run 400-600 words and are pulled from areas like economics, philosophy, science, sociology, and business. There's no way to predict topics, so the goal isn't subject knowledge — it's comprehension speed and the ability to track an argument.

  • Read for structure, not detail. As you read each paragraph, note 2-3 keywords capturing its role in the passage (example, counter-argument, conclusion). This saves you from re-reading the whole passage for every question.
  • Don't skip dense passages. Difficult-sounding passages often have the most direct questions, because the author has less room to bury tricky inferences in complex sentences.
  • Target 7-8 minutes per passage including all its questions, once you're past the early practice phase.
  • Build daily reading stamina with long-form sources — editorials, essays, and analytical articles work better than novels because they match CAT's argumentative style.

Verbal Ability: Para Jumbles & Para Summary

VA questions test logical flow, not grammar or vocabulary — there are no direct grammar or vocabulary questions in CAT anymore.

  • Para Jumbles: Look for the opening sentence first (usually introduces the topic without pronouns referring backward), then find linking words (however, therefore, this) that reveal sequence.
  • Para Summary: Pick the option that captures the core argument, not just a detail mentioned in the passage. Eliminate options that are too narrow or too broad first.
  • Odd Sentence Out: Identify the paragraph's central theme, then find the sentence that drifts from it or repeats a point already made.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Chasing speed before accuracy. A rushed first read that misses the passage's core argument costs more time than a slightly slower, focused read.
  2. Ignoring the error log. Not tracking why you got a question wrong (overthinking vs. missed evidence vs. extreme options) means repeating the same mistakes in every mock.
  3. Skipping VA to "save time" for RC. Para Jumbles and Para Summary are often more scorable than a tough RC question — don't abandon them under time pressure.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many questions should I attempt in VARC to get a good percentile?

Around 18-20 correct attempts out of ~24 questions is generally considered a strong VARC performance. High accuracy matters more than attempting everything.

Do I need to study grammar and vocabulary for CAT VARC?

No. CAT VARC does not test grammar or vocabulary directly — the entire section is passage and paragraph-based, so comprehension and logical flow matter more than word lists.

How long does it take to improve VARC score?

With consistent daily reading and RC practice, most aspirants see measurable improvement in 3-4 months, though building genuine reading speed can take longer if starting from a low base.
