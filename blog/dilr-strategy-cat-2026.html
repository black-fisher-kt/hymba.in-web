DILR Strategy for CAT 2026: How to Pick & Solve Sets Fast

Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

DILR has a reputation as CAT's toughest section — but the honest truth is that most aspirants lose marks not from lack of preparation, but from poor set selection. Picking the wrong set to attempt first can cost you 15-20 minutes and an entire other set's worth of marks.

Set Selection: The Real Skill

This is the single biggest lever in DILR performance. Here's the approach toppers consistently use:

  • Scan all sets first (3-5 minutes). Before solving anything, skim every set to gauge which look familiar or manageable versus which have too many variables or an unclear starting point.
  • Categorise by difficulty — easy, moderate, hard — based on your first impression, not by attempting them.
  • Start with your most confident set, not necessarily the "easiest-looking" one — familiarity with the format matters more than apparent simplicity.
  • Set a hard cap of 3-4 minutes to decide whether a set is worth attempting. If you can't see a starting point by then, move on.
  • Abandon a set if you're 12+ minutes in without progress — that time is better spent securing a different set fully.

Solving 2 full sets with high accuracy consistently outperforms half-solving 4 sets. Quality of attempts beats quantity here.

Common DILR Topics

  • Arrangements — linear, circular, matrix-based seating and grouping puzzles
  • Data tables, bar graphs, line graphs, and pie charts
  • Games & Tournaments — tricky but highly scoring once you understand the scoring logic
  • Venn diagrams and set-based reasoning
  • Network/route-based sets and scheduling problems

DILR is the most volatile section year to year — you genuinely can't predict whether a paper will lean more DI-heavy or LR-heavy, so prepare both equally rather than favouring one.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Attempting the first set you see. Scanning all sets before starting is non-negotiable, even under time pressure.
  2. Not tracking error type. Split errors into set-selection errors (chose a hard set unnecessarily), constraint errors (misread a condition), and overtime errors (spent too long) — each needs a different fix.
  3. Refusing to abandon a set. Sunk-cost thinking ("I've already spent 10 minutes") often costs more marks than switching would.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many DILR sets should I solve to get a good percentile?

Solving 2 to 2.5 sets with full accuracy (roughly 6-8 correct questions) is typically enough for a 95+ percentile in this section.

Is DILR the same as pure Logical Reasoning?

No. CAT doesn't separate DI and LR into different sections — they appear interwoven within the same sets, and a single set may require both data interpretation and logical reasoning skills.

How many DILR sets should I practice daily while preparing?

Most experts recommend 2-3 quality sets a day, focusing on accuracy and pattern recognition before increasing speed.
